# Data assumptions

Everything in `data/` is generated by `make world` and is gitignored. Only this
file is committed.

**All data is synthetic.** There are no real names, phone numbers, NIDs,
addresses or other personal data. All IDs are random strings. Locations sit on a
made-up flat map in km, not real geography. Results on this data show that the
method works. They do not prove real-world accuracy.

## How to read this file

Every number lives in `configs/world.yaml` or `configs/patterns.yaml`. This file
explains **why** each one was chosen and where it comes from. To change a
number, edit the config. A test (`ml/tests/test_world_summary.py`) checks that
every config section and every pattern is mentioned here.

Source tags:

- **[brief]**: a domain fact from the team brief (CLAUDE.md, "Domain facts").
  It comes from our project research and should be cited from the original
  documents in the final report.
- **[assumption]**: a team choice to make a plausible synthetic world. It is not
  measured from real upay data, which we do not have. It should be checked for
  plausibility, not treated as fact.

Actual counts produced by these settings are in `reports/world_summary.md`
(written by code).

## world.yaml

### `seed`
Master random seed (42). The `JACHAI_SEED` env var or `--seed` overrides it.
Each part of the generator draws from its own stream derived from the seed and
the part's name, so the same seed always gives the same world, and switching one
pattern off does not change the random draws of the others.

### `calendar`
- 120 days, 1 Jul to 28 Oct 2026. **[assumption]** Long enough for monthly
  rhythms and a before/after comparison around the regime change.
- `regime_change` on 1 Oct 2026. **[brief]** From this date Bangla QR MDR and
  IRF are zero, merchants get instant credit, and an incentive applies to
  payments up to Tk 2,000 (splitting prohibited).

### `regulation`
- `cash_out_limit_daily`: Tk 30,000. **[brief]** Bangladesh Bank cash-out limit
  referenced in reporting. Used by the `limit_bypass` pattern.
- `qr_incentive_cap`: Tk 2,000. **[brief]** Used by `incentive_splitting`.

### `scenario`
- P2P Bangla QR starts on 1 Nov 2026 **[brief]**, after our window. The
  `p2p_qr_enabled` switch (off by default; `make world WORLD_ARGS=--p2p` turns it on)
  is a **what-if**: it pretends P2P QR starts on `p2p_qr_start` (15 Oct) so we can
  generate the `p2p_transfers` table and the `p2p_disguise` pattern. Results from
  this scenario are labelled as such.

### `population`
3,000 shops, 30,000 customers, 600 agents. **[assumption]** Large enough for a
peer-group analysis per category and area. Small enough to generate in seconds
on a laptop.

### `map`
A 300 × 300 km square. Dhaka zones lie within 12 km of the centre, district
towns are spread across the map, and rural haat zones lie within 25 km of a
district town. **[assumption]** Gives realistic distance contrasts ("payer lives
60 km away") without using real places.

### `areas`
Three area types: `dhaka_urban` (20 zones, 40% of shops), `district_town`
(40 zones, 35%), `rural_haat` (60 zones, 25%). **[assumption]**
- QR volume multipliers: 1.3 in Dhaka, 1.0 in towns, 0.6 rural. We assume
  digital payments are more common in cities.
- Rural zones hold weekly haat (market) days, 2 per week, used by
  `hn_haat_day_spike`.

### `size_tiers`
Small (60% of shops, 0.5× volume, 30–80 regular customers), medium (30%, 1×,
80–200), large (10%, 2.5×, 200–500). **[assumption]** Most shops in Bangladesh
are small.

### `categories`
Seven categories, each with a share of shops, a QR payment rate and a ticket-size
distribution. **[assumption]** Ticket sizes are lognormal (most purchases small,
a few large), clipped to a min and max:

| Category | Shops | QR payments/day (medium shop, town) | Median ticket |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| grocery | 32% | 10 | Tk 300 |
| tea_stall | 20% | 14 | Tk 40 |
| pharmacy | 14% | 6 | Tk 350 |
| mobile_phone_shop | 10% | 5 | Tk 500 |
| clothing | 13% | 3 | Tk 1,200 |
| electronics | 6% | 1.5 | Tk 6,000 |
| wholesaler | 5% | 3 | Tk 9,000 |

- Prices are rounded to Tk 5. A share of larger tickets are rounded to Tk 100
  (Tk 500 for electronics and wholesalers), because shop prices are often round.
  Electronics and wholesalers have the highest share of round tickets (45–50%),
  so "round amount" alone is not proof of misuse.
- Each category has opening hours (for example tea stalls 06–23, wholesalers
  06–18). Payments outside them count as after-hours.

### `payers`
Who pays an honest shop **[assumption]**: 65% the shop's own regular customers,
25% other people from the same zone, 10% anyone. 2% of honest payments happen
after hours (late shoppers, shop owners settling up).

### `qr`
- 1 to 5 QR codes per shop. **[brief]** One merchant account can carry 4–5
  Bangla QR codes from different providers.
- 55% of QR payments are "on-us" (payer wallet is upay). **[assumption]**

### `weekly`
Fridays at 0.85× volume, and the first 5 days of each month (salary days) at
1.1×. **[assumption]**

### `remittances`
12% of customers receive remittances, about one a month, median Tk 20,000.
Corridors: Gulf 60%, Europe 15%, Asia-Pacific 15%, North America 10%.
**[assumption]** The Gulf is the largest source of remittances to Bangladesh;
the exact split is illustrative.

### `add_money`
About 3 top-ups per customer a month, median Tk 1,500. Channels: agent cash-in
60%, bank 25%, card 15%. Cash-in goes through an agent in the customer's zone.
**[assumption]**

### `p2p`
Only when the P2P scenario is on. About 2 transfers per customer a month,
median Tk 1,000, 70% to someone in the same zone. **[assumption]**

## patterns.yaml

Each pattern is a separate function registered by name. A shop gets at most one
pattern, so each shop has an unambiguous true label. Pattern sizes are given as
shares of all shops, so they scale with the world. All values are
**[assumption]** unless tagged.

### Label noise
`case_label` is what past analyst cases would show. It is imperfect:
- Shops: 30% of misuse shops were never caught. 1% of honest shops were wrongly
  flagged.
- Payments (QR and P2P): 30% missed, 0.1% wrongly flagged. The false-flag rate is
  lower because there are about 800 times more payments than shops; at 1%, false
  flags would outnumber real misuse.

Models may train on `case_label`. Evaluation uses the hidden `_true_is_misuse`.

### Misuse patterns (true label 1)

1. `round_amount_cash_desk`: 1.5% of shops (grocery, mobile phone shops,
   pharmacies, tea stalls) hand out cash for fake QR purchases. Amounts are
   round, Tk 1,000 to 20,000. Half of the payments are after hours. Payers come
   from a pool of 5% of customers who use cash desks repeatedly, and 60% of them
   live outside the shop's zone. Half topped up from a bank 10 minutes to
   6 hours before (bank → wallet → "purchase" → cash). These shops act as
   unlicensed cash-out points. **[brief]**
2. `remittance_drain`: 15% of remittance receivers pay 92–100% of a remittance
   to a colluding shop at least 60 km away, within 1–48 hours, in 1–3 payments,
   then their account goes quiet. Colluding shops are 0.8% of shops. **[brief]**
3. `turnover_burst`: 1% of shops, all small, suddenly receive Tk 3–7 lakh on each
   of 2–5 days, from payers at least 40 km away. **[brief]** (Tk 5-lakh-scale
   bursts)
4. `limit_bypass`: 5 rings per 1,000 shops. A ring is 3–6 nearby shops. 5–12
   customers per ring each move Tk 32,000–60,000 in a day, above the Tk 30,000
   limit, split across several ring shops within a few hours, on 3–10 days.
   **[brief]**
5. `incentive_splitting`: 1.3% of shops (not electronics or wholesalers, which
   rarely sell under Tk 2,000). From 1 Oct only, 3–10 colluding payers per shop
   make 3–8 payments a day of Tk 1,800–1,999, just under the incentive cap.
   **[brief]**
6. `p2p_disguise`: only in the P2P what-if scenario. 1.3% of shops ask regular
   customers to pay the owner's personal wallet instead of the merchant QR:
   5–20 transfers a day at normal ticket sizes. Half of the shop's own QR sales
   move to P2P. **[brief]** (reverse risk after 1 Nov)

### Hard negatives (honest look-alikes, true label 0)

- `hn_big_ticket_retail`: half of electronics shops and wholesalers make about
  0.4 extra large sales a day, Tk 5,000–50,000 rounded to Tk 1,000, to their
  regular buyers during opening hours. Looks like a cash desk by amount alone.
  **[brief]**
- `hn_festival_spike`: honest shops sell more during a festival (clothing 2.5×,
  electronics 1.6×, grocery 1.4×, others 1.2×). **No Eid falls in Jul–Oct 2026**
  (Eid al-Adha is around late May), so we use **Durga Puja, about 16–21 Oct 2026**.
  These dates are approximate and must be checked before the final report.
  The brief mentions Eid spikes; the same logic applies to any festival.
- `hn_haat_day_spike`: rural haat shops double their volume on their zone's weekly
  market days. **[brief]**
- `hn_new_shop_ramp`: 5% of shops open during the window (20–70% of the way in)
  and ramp from zero to full volume over 30 days. Looks like a burst. **[brief]**

## Known limits

- The misuse share of shops and of QR payment value (exact figures in
  `reports/world_summary.md`) is probably higher than in reality. We chose it so
  the models have enough examples to learn from. Precision on real data would be
  lower at a lower base rate.
- Patterns are cleaner than real behaviour. Real misuse mixes tactics and adapts.
  The evaluation plan answers this with a held-out pattern and an evasion test.
- No customer churn, no app outages and no seasonality beyond weekly, salary-day,
  haat and festival effects.
- Honest customers' behaviour does not depend on their own past (no learning or
  habits beyond regular-shop pools).
