# Jachai (যাচাই): merchant-transaction integrity for Bangla QR

**Team DIU Comebackhobena** — K.M. Sajidul Haq (Captain), Tanjimul Astak Siam,
Mohammed Muntasir Rahman Joy · AI DEV FEST 2026 AI Hackathon (DIU CPC × upay) ·
Track 05, Merchant & Agent Intelligence, with a Trust & Risk engine ·
Live: <https://jachai-eta.vercel.app> · Code: <https://github.com/kmsajid044-ship-it/jachai>

## 1. Problem

A customer "pays" a shop Tk 10,000 by Bangla QR, buys nothing, and walks out with
about Tk 9,815 in cash. The shop works as an unlicensed cash-out point: the
customer bypasses the Tk 30,000 daily cash-out limit and leaves no trail, and upay
loses agent cash-out fees (Tk 13–18.50 per Tk 1,000) while paying interchange on
off-us QR. From 1 Oct 2026 Bangla QR merchant fees fall to zero with an incentive
on payments up to Tk 2,000, which also invites payment splitting; from 1 Nov 2026
P2P Bangla QR creates the reverse risk of business income disguised as personal
transfers. Acquirers are expected to monitor merchants with unusual patterns and
stop splitting and cash-outs. A blanket limit on every shop is the blunt answer,
and it hurts honest wholesalers, electronics sellers and festival traders most.

*Sources:* team project brief and domain notes (repo `CLAUDE.md`, "Domain facts"),
drawn from the organisers' challenge material [1]. The primary regulatory
documents should be cited directly in any version beyond this hackathon.

## 2. Idea

Don't limit every shop. For every payment and every shop, answer one question:
**real commerce, or a hidden cash-out?** Rank the few shops that need review,
explain why in plain English and Bangla, leave every decision to a human analyst,
and turn confirmed high-demand cash-out points into licensed upay agents.

## 3. Implemented solution

All data is synthetic and generated by code (`make world`): 3,000 shops, 30,000
customers and 600 agents over 120 days (Jul–Oct 2026), including the 1 Oct rule
change. Six misuse patterns (round-amount cash desk, remittance drain, turnover
burst, limit-bypass rings, incentive splitting, P2P disguise) and eight honest
look-alikes (big-ticket sellers, bulk buys, festival and haat-day spikes, new
shops) are injected by pluggable functions. True labels stay in hidden columns
used only for evaluation; models see only rule votes and sparse, dated,
shop-level past cases (7% of shops, biased towards rule-flagged shops, with wrong
verdicts).

The system has three scores fused into one band:

1. **Payment score**: LightGBM on soft targets (rule votes plus past cases),
   isotonic calibration, threshold frozen on validation.
2. **Shop score**: expected-turnover regression by category, area and size
   (robust z-score) plus an Isolation Forest within each peer group.
3. **Network score**: payer–shop graph (NetworkX); shops linked when one payer
   passes the daily limit across three or more shops on the same day; Louvain
   communities and a ring flag.

Fusion is an equal-weight average of component ranks (rules included as one
input), giving a low / review / high band with cut-offs frozen on validation. A
FastAPI service and a Next.js dashboard serve the alert queue, case view,
merchant notice, policy simulator and Trust page; a demo mode reads bundled JSON
produced by the real API if the API is unreachable.

## 4. Key features

- Alert queue labelled "needs review", never a verdict; case view with three
  scores, top-3 reasons in English and Bangla, 30-day timeline and linked-shop graph.
- Decisions (clear, monitor, educate, convert, restrict, escalate) require a
  reason and go to an append-only, hash-chained SQLite log; nothing is blocked
  automatically.
- Polite Bangla merchant notice built from the shop's own reasons, with an
  appeal form preview.
- Policy simulator: the same month replayed under five policies with sliders for
  misuse share, fee rate, analyst capacity and limit level.
- Trust page: results vs baselines, evasion and ring results, fairness slices,
  data card and limitations.

## 5. AI approach

- **Weak supervision.** Eight labelling functions (one per typology plus two
  "honest" ones) vote; a weighted vote behind a replaceable interface combines
  them. Trained on rule votes alone, the model only reproduced the rules, so
  sparse dated past cases were added as higher-weight evidence.
- **Point-in-time features.** 43 payment, payer and shop features use only
  information available at payment time; a cut-off test proves it.
- **Explanations.** Exact TreeSHAP values from LightGBM and exact fusion
  contributions are mapped to reason codes; every number in a reason is filled in
  by code, and a missing value raises an error instead of becoming zero.
- **Evaluation protocol.** Split by shop and by time; one pattern
  (`turnover_burst`) held out of training entirely; thresholds frozen on
  validation; a single-feature leakage check (all features below 0.95 strength
  after the synthetic world was made less easy); the test set accessed once and
  logged in `reports/test_access_log.md`.

## 6. Results (synthetic data only)

**Validation, three seeds (mean ± std), shop level.** Jachai with rules reached
PR-AUC 0.774 ± 0.059 against 0.899 ± 0.033 for rules alone and 0.137 ± 0.019 for a
blanket limit. Under evasion (misuse amounts made non-round, system not retrained)
Jachai held up better: PR-AUC 0.599 ± 0.024 vs 0.588 ± 0.051, value recall
0.893 ± 0.013 vs 0.739 ± 0.053, and limit-bypass ring recall 0.591 vs 0.024. The
agreed success criteria for adding rules to fusion were **not all met**: evasion
and rings passed, the normal-world criterion (within 0.02 PR-AUC of rules) did not.

**Final test, single access** (615 test shops, 71 misuse, matched 70-shop budget):

| method | PR-AUC | precision | recall | misuse-value recall | honest shops flagged | held-out pattern recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jachai with rules | 0.584 | 0.614 | 0.606 | 0.774 | 27 | 0.133 |
| Rules only | 0.843 | 0.843 | 0.831 | 0.894 | 11 | 0.600 |
| Blanket limit | 0.486 | 0.371 | 0.366 | 0.549 | 44 | 0.700 |

**Honest reading.** On this synthetic test, rules were stronger than Jachai,
including on the held-out pattern, and Jachai flagged more honest shops than the
rules (but fewer than a blanket limit). Caveats: the world and the rules were
written from the same typologies, which favours rules (circularity); counts are
small (29–43 misuse shops per validation seed, 71 at test); one evasion strategy
was tested; no real data was used. Jachai's measured advantage is robustness when
misusers adapt and network detection of rings, not normal-world accuracy.

## 7. Real-life impact

The intended value is prioritisation: limited analyst time goes to the shops with
the strongest combined evidence; honest big sellers are not caught by a single
city-wide cap; confirmed high-demand cash-out points become agent leads, moving
cash-out into a licensed, fee-earning channel. In the fast-profile simulator
replay (300 shops, 15 days; a diagnostic, not a forecast) a Tk 100,000 blanket
limit restricted 12 honest shops and blocked Tk 2.0 million of genuine sales while
stopping 25% of misuse taka; rules and Jachai each stopped about 73% with no
honest shop restricted.

## 8. Responsible AI

No real personal data; random IDs; a test scans for phone- or NID-like values.
Hidden truth is never served by the API. The system recommends and a named analyst
decides, with a written reason, in an append-only log. Merchant wording is polite
and non-accusatory, with an appeal path. Fairness: validation false-alarm rates by
area type, shop size and category are shown with sample sizes. All results are
labelled synthetic.

## 9. Limitations and next steps

Synthetic data and circularity limit every claim; small slices make some rates
unstable; only one evasion strategy is tested; latency and security are prototype
grade (SQLite, in-memory scoring). Next: a pilot on real, consented data with
analyst-audited labels; a learned label model; more evasion strategies; recalibration
and fairness review on real merchants; production storage and access control.
